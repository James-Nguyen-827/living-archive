---
title: Designing with edges
summary: What eco-brutalist buildings teach us about boundaries, shelter, and honest interfaces.
publishDate: 2026-08-18
tags: [architecture, interfaces]
---

Eco-brutalist architecture is compelling when structure and ecology negotiate rather than decorate one another. A planted wall is not automatically ecological; the interesting work happens where shade, water, wind, maintenance, and occupation meet.

Interfaces have edges too. They decide what is protected, what is exposed, and when a visitor can move from one state into another. Making those boundaries legible can feel more humane than making everything frictionless.
