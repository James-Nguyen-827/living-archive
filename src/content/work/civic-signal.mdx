## The situation

City information often arrives as a wall of policy language, scattered portals, and maps that assume specialist knowledge. Civic Signal began with a narrower question: what does a resident need to understand before they can confidently act?

The work combined field interviews, information architecture, and rapid prototypes. Instead of organizing around municipal departments, the interface organizes around the decisions people are trying to make.

## Working from the decision outward

I mapped recurring questions, then traced each answer back to its source. This reversed the usual hierarchy. Details remained available, but only after a clear summary, timeframe, and next action.

> The interface should make complexity legible without pretending the underlying system is simple.

## System, not screens

The resulting pattern library covers alerts, maps, evidence trails, and uncertainty. Every component includes a plain-language version and preserves a link to the source record. Prototypes were evaluated with keyboard-only navigation and at 200% zoom from the first round.

## Outcome

Testing showed faster task completion and fewer interpretation errors. More importantly, the system gave the content team a repeatable way to publish future topics without rebuilding the interface each time.
