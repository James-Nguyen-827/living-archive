## A catalog with consequences

Material Cycles documents building components that can be repaired, recovered, and reused. The initial brief asked for a searchable database. Research showed the bigger opportunity was helping people understand the life around each object: where it came from, what it could become, and which trade-offs mattered.

## From inventory to landscape

The visual system borrows the precision of a parts ledger but avoids making every item feel interchangeable. Dense indexes support known-item retrieval; narrative pathways let visitors follow a material through extraction, use, recovery, and recombination.

## Designing for incomplete evidence

Not every material had perfect data. Rather than hide those gaps, the interface gives provenance and confidence their own visual language. This made uncertainty inspectable and prevented unverified environmental claims from looking equivalent to measured ones.

## Outcome

The catalog now works as a sourcing tool, workshop aid, and small exhibition interface. Its component model continues to support new material families without flattening their differences.
